Serious Muay Thai training is one of the more popular soft-power-adjacent reasons foreigners stay long-term in Thailand. Here's how the ED visa route works for it.

## What is the Muay Thai ED visa?

Thailand's Education Visa, obtained through enrolment at a registered Muay Thai gym rather than a traditional school — initial validity of 90 days, with extensions available every 90 days for up to a year.

## Is the Muay Thai ED visa still available?

Yes — it remains available alongside the newer DTV visa option, and continues to be a popular choice for committed Muay Thai trainees specifically.

## How much does a Muay Thai ED visa cost?

Full 12-month training packages run **25,000–60,000 THB**, with Chiang Mai gyms typically falling at the lower-to-mid end, around **25,000–40,000 THB** for the full year. Extension fees add **1,900 THB per 90-day cycle**.

## Training requirements on the Muay Thai ED visa

**8–15 hours per week** of training is generally expected, with attendance monitored by the gym.

## Muay Thai ED visa application process

### Step 1: choose a licensed gym and enrol

The gym must hold proper Ministry of Education registration — not every gym offering training packages is set up to sponsor this visa.

### Step 2: apply for the initial Non-Immigrant ED visa

Typically processed at a Thai embassy before you travel.

### Step 3: extend every 90 days

At your local immigration office, with enrolment and attendance documentation from your gym.

## Muay Thai ED visa vs DTV visa for training

If your primary purpose is genuinely serious Muay Thai training with a specific gym, the ED visa is purpose-built for that. If you also have foreign remote income and want more flexibility, the DTV may suit you better — see our [DTV vs ED comparison context](/dtv-visa-chiang-mai/) for the broader picture.
