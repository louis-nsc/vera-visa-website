The Thailand marriage visa (Non-Immigrant O) lets foreign spouses of Thai nationals live in Thailand long-term, with indefinite annual renewal. Here's the complete picture — requirements, documents, process, and what sets it apart from other long-stay options.

## What is the Thailand marriage visa (Non-Immigrant O)?

The Non-Immigrant O visa, granted on the basis of marriage to a Thai national, allows a one-year stay with indefinite annual renewal as long as the marriage remains valid and you continue to meet the financial requirements.

## Key requirements in brief

- Legal marriage to a Thai national, registered in Thailand or legalised if married abroad
- Financial evidence — see below
- Valid passport with sufficient remaining validity
- Clean criminal record

## Financial requirements explained

One of two routes:

- **Bank deposit:** **400,000 THB** held in a Thai bank account, with a seasoning period before application
- **Monthly income:** **40,000 THB** in verified foreign income transferred to Thailand each month

A combination of both, proportionally, is also accepted if neither threshold is met individually.

## The marriage visa vs. other long-term options

Unlike the retirement visa, the marriage visa has no minimum age requirement and a lower financial threshold (400,000 THB vs. 800,000 THB). Unlike the DTV, it requires an actual marriage to a Thai national rather than remote income — it isn't a visa you can simply qualify for through work arrangements.

## Step-by-step application process

1. Register your marriage at a Thai district office (or legalise a foreign marriage certificate)
2. Open a Thai bank account and meet the financial threshold, or gather income evidence
3. Gather identity, marriage, and financial documents
4. Submit your application at Thai immigration
5. Attend any required interview or home visit
6. Receive approval and register your address

## The Thai marriage documents requirement

- Official Thai marriage certificate (Kor Ror 3), or a foreign marriage certificate legalised and translated into Thai
- Thai spouse's national ID card and house registration book
- Your passport, valid 18+ months
- Financial evidence — bank letter or embassy income letter

## Immigration home visits: what to expect

Some immigration offices conduct a home visit as part of the application or renewal process, confirming the couple genuinely lives together at the registered address. This isn't universal, but applicants should be prepared for it.

## Work rights for marriage visa holders

Unlike the retirement visa, marriage visa holders can legally work in Thailand — but only with a separate work permit obtained through an employer. The marriage visa itself doesn't include work authorisation; it's the correct base visa to then apply for one.

## Pathway to permanent residency (PR)

Holding a marriage-based Non-Immigrant O visa for a sufficient continuous period is one of the recognised pathways toward Thai permanent residency, for those who eventually want a more permanent status than annual visa renewal.

## Key differences from DTV visa

The DTV is income/activity-based and available to a broad range of applicants without a Thai spouse; the marriage visa requires an actual marriage and offers a pathway toward longer-term residency status the DTV doesn't provide.

## Getting started: initial application timeline

From gathering documents to approval, the process typically spans **4–6 weeks**, depending on how quickly marriage certificate legalisation (if applicable) and financial evidence can be assembled.
