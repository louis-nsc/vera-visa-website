The Thailand marriage visa requires documentation from both spouses. Here's the complete checklist — what you need, what your Thai spouse needs, and what trips people up most often.

## Overview: two sets of requirements

Your application needs evidence from you (the foreign spouse) and your Thai spouse together — missing or mismatched documents from either side is the most common reason for delay.

## Financial requirements in detail

One of two thresholds:

- **400,000 THB bank balance option:** held in a Thai bank account for 2–3 months without withdrawal
- **40,000 THB monthly income option:** verified through embassy documentation showing at least 6 months of regular deposits

## Your (foreign spouse) documentation

- Valid passport — 18+ months remaining for initial applications, 6+ months for renewals
- Completed TM.87 application form, signed by both spouses
- Four recent passport-style photographs, 4×6 cm
- Official marriage certificate with certified copies
- Bank passbook with certified statements, or an embassy income letter

## Thai spouse documentation

- Thai national ID card copies (both sides)
- Certified copy of the house registration book (Tabian Baan)
- Signature on all application forms

## Marriage documentation specifics

A Thai-registered marriage certificate uses the Kor Ror 2/3 format. If you married abroad, your foreign marriage certificate needs to be legalised and translated into Thai before it's accepted.

## The couple photos requirement

Immigration generally expects **3–5 photos of the couple together**, taken within the past six months, showing you both indoors and outdoors at your registered address — evidence of genuine cohabitation, not just the marriage itself.

## Initial application document list

Passport, application form, photos, marriage certificate, financial evidence, and your Thai spouse's ID and house registration — assembled together as a single, complete package.

## Annual renewal document list

Largely the same set, refreshed: updated financial evidence (with the same 2–3 month seasoning), a current passport (6+ months remaining is sufficient at renewal), and updated couple photos if requested.

## Common documentation mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Name mismatches** between your passport, bank documents, and marriage certificate
- **Insufficient bank seasoning** — withdrawing before the required 2–3 month period has passed
- **Missing or outdated couple photos** that don't clearly show cohabitation
- **Untranslated foreign marriage certificates** submitted without the required Thai translation

## Document organisation tips

Submit everything as a single, clearly labelled package, with your documents and your spouse's documents clearly separated but cross-referenced — this is exactly how we prepare every client's submission.

## Timeline for document preparation

Allow **4–6 weeks**, longer if your marriage certificate needs legalisation and translation from abroad.

## Final document checklist before submitting

Confirm: bank seasoning period is satisfied, all names match exactly across every document, your Thai spouse's documents are current, and your couple photos clearly show the required cohabitation evidence.
