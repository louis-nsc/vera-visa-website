# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Global expats — retirees, digital nomads, students, spouses, business owners, mostly from the US, UK, Australia, and Canada — researching Thai visas, often from a phone, often anxious about a confusing, high-stakes bureaucratic process. Many are older or non-expert in immigration law; plain, internationally-neutral language matters. The job to be done: figure out which visa applies to them, understand the real requirements and cost, and decide whether to hand the process to Vera Visa.

## Product Purpose

The marketing site for Vera Visa, a visa agency in Chiang Mai, Thailand. It exists to convert an anxious researcher into a WhatsApp conversation ("Chat on WhatsApp →" / "Book a free consultation") by demonstrating expert precision on visa types (DTV, Education, Marriage, Retirement, Guardian, Business/Work Permit, Volunteer/Non-O). Success is measured in qualified WhatsApp chats and SEO rankings that outperform the legacy WordPress site being replaced.

## Brand Personality

A trusted local expert who has done this a thousand times: calm, warm, human, precise. The tone is expert-friend, never salesy — speaks as "we" (the agency) to "you" (the applicant). Specificity is the core trust signal: real numbers (800,000 THB, 65,000 THB/month, "4–6 weeks"), never vague placeholders like "requirements vary." No emoji — precision comes from the monospace "official document" language, not decoration.

## Anti-references

- **Cold blue corporate-agency template.** No corporate-blue palettes, no generic enterprise-SaaS chrome.
- **Delicate boutique law-firm look.** No precious serif-on-cream minimalism that reads fragile or overly formal — the brand is warm and approachable, not white-glove.
- Icon-grid services sections with generic line icons (services are status cards, not icon tiles — see DESIGN.md/DS README).
- Full-bleed stock photography (handshakes, passports, airports) — real Chiang Mai/team photography only, if any.

## Design Principles

- **Specificity is a trust signal.** Concrete numbers and timeframes are part of the visual design, not just copy — never leave them vague.
- **The document metaphor, not the SaaS card.** The system should feel like a printed/stamped visa document (status cards, perforation motif, monospace data fields), not a floating dashboard card. 4px radius, no shadows, separation via hairline borders only.
- **Orange marks one thing, never a wash.** `--signal` (#FF630F) highlights a single button, status, or word — it is a stamp, not a background.
- **Calm under stress.** The audience reads carefully while anxious; generous spacing (never crowded), plain language, alternating paper/sand rhythm instead of gray dividers or visual noise.
- **Explain, don't assume.** Thailand-specific terms (DTV, O-A, 90-day report) get explained inline for an international-newcomer audience.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA baseline. Particularly relevant given older retirees and non-native English readers in the audience — body text contrast, readable type sizes, and plain language all matter more here than on a typical SaaS marketing site. Reduced-motion alternatives required for any animation.
