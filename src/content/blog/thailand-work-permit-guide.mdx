Any foreigner working in Thailand needs a work permit, with no exceptions for remote arrangements, short engagements, or unpaid "favors." Here's the complete picture for 2026: who needs one, what your employer must provide, and what happens if you work without one.

## What is a Thai work permit and who needs one?

A work permit is official government authorisation for a foreign national to perform specific, legally sanctioned work in Thailand. It's always paired with a Non-Immigrant B visa; neither works without the other. Anyone performing work activities in Thailand needs one, regardless of whether they're paid in Thailand or abroad.

## The critical rule: no work without permission

Thai law defines "work" broadly: it covers far more than traditional employment. Even unpaid activities can be considered work requiring authorisation in some circumstances. The safest approach is always to confirm your specific situation before starting any activity in Thailand.

## The Non-Immigrant B visa requirement

The work permit application requires you to already hold (or be applying for) a Non-Immigrant B visa. The two processes typically run in parallel, with your employer sponsoring both.

## Thailand's new e-work permit system (2025)

An online e-work permit system was introduced in 2025, streamlining what was previously a more paper-heavy process. Applications can now be submitted and tracked digitally for most standard cases.

## Employer requirements for sponsoring a work permit

Your sponsoring company must meet:

- **Minimum paid-up capital:** 2,000,000 THB per foreign worker employed
- **Thai-to-foreign worker ratio:** 4:1 standard, or 2:1 for BOI-promoted companies (as of October 2025)
- Registration with the Department of Business Development
- Current tax compliance and Social Security registration

## The application process: who does what

Your employer provides company registration, capital evidence, and payroll documentation; you provide passport, qualifications, medical certificate, and a clean criminal record check. Both sets are submitted together.

## Processing time and timeline

- **Standard processing:** 2–4 weeks
- **BOI-promoted companies:** 1–2 weeks, reflecting the streamlined process available to them

## Work permit fees

- **Application fee:** 100 THB
- **First-year issuance:** 3,000 THB
- **Annual renewal:** 1,500 THB

## BOI-promoted companies and streamlined process

Companies promoted by Thailand's Board of Investment benefit from a reduced employee ratio requirement (2:1 instead of 4:1) and faster processing, a meaningful advantage for foreign-invested businesses structured to qualify.

## The Non-B visa and work permit coordination

These two approvals need to stay aligned throughout your employment. A work permit renewal generally needs to happen alongside your visa extension, not independently of it.

## Restricted professions and occupations

Thai law reserves certain professions for Thai nationals only. We confirm whether your intended role is open to foreign workers before any application begins.

## Work permit duration and renewal

Work permits are valid for one year from issuance and must be renewed annually alongside your visa, with updated company and personal documentation each time.

## The e-work permit system: how to apply online

The digital system allows employers to submit and track applications without the in-person visits previously required for most standard cases, a meaningful time saving compared to the older paper process.

## Why work permits are genuinely enforced

Penalties are real and actively enforced:

- **Employer fines:** 10,000–20,000 THB per day, per unauthorised foreign worker
- **Worker fines:** 5,000–10,000 THB, with potential deportation

This isn't a formality that's loosely policed. Both employer and employee carry meaningful legal exposure for non-compliance.

## Remote workers and work permits

Working remotely for a foreign employer or clients while in Thailand on a DTV doesn't require a Thai work permit; that's specifically what the DTV is designed to allow. A work permit is required specifically for work *for a Thai company* or Thai-sourced income.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I work for a foreign company while living in Thailand without a work permit?** If you're on a DTV visa working for a foreign employer or clients, this is permitted without a Thai work permit. Working for a Thai company always requires one, regardless of your visa type.

**What's the Thai-to-foreign employee ratio?** 4 Thai employees per foreign worker is standard; BOI-promoted companies benefit from a reduced 2:1 ratio as of October 2025.

**How much does it cost overall?** Combined application and first-year issuance fees total roughly 3,100 THB, with 1,500 THB for each annual renewal. See our [full cost guide](/thailand-work-permit-requirements/) for the complete breakdown including document costs.
