Studying at an approved language school is one of the most accessible routes to the ED visa in Chiang Mai. Here's what qualifies, what to watch for, and the full process.

## What is the ED visa and who is it for?

A Non-Immigrant ED visa obtained through enrolment at a Ministry of Education-registered school, covering Thai language, and several other approved program types described below.

## The 20,000 THB per year school fee requirement: what it means

Qualifying schools must charge **at least 20,000 THB annually**: this minimum fee requirement is part of how immigration distinguishes legitimate education providers from informal arrangements.

## Types of study programs that qualify for an ED visa in Chiang Mai

Thai language instruction, Muay Thai training, traditional Thai massage study, yoga, and cooking courses all qualify, provided the school maintains proper Ministry of Education registration.

## What to look for in a Chiang Mai language school (avoid scams)

Red flags include suspiciously low fees, promises of minimal participation requirements, an absence of physical classrooms, or marketing that emphasises rapid visa processing over actual educational substance. A legitimate school will have real classes, real attendance tracking, and won't promise to minimise your study commitment.

## The application process: from school enrolment to immigration

1. Enrol at an approved institution
2. Obtain your enrolment documentation
3. Submit materials to immigration
4. Receive your visa stamp
5. Begin classes within 30 days

## How long can you stay? Renewals and extensions explained

Initial visas typically run **90 days**, with extensions providing **1-year periods** as long as enrolment continues and your attendance record stays satisfactory, and schools report attendance directly to immigration officials.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I work while studying on an ED visa?** No, employment is prohibited under this visa category, regardless of the program type.

**What happens if my attendance drops below requirements?** Schools are required to report attendance: falling short can jeopardise your visa renewal, so treat your study schedule as a genuine commitment, not a formality.
