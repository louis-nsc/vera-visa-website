Thailand doesn't have a dedicated "volunteer visa" category — instead, volunteers use the Non-Immigrant O visa, the same category used for retirement and marriage visas, adapted to a volunteer sponsorship basis.

## Tourist visa or Non-Immigrant O? Choosing the right route

Which route you need depends entirely on how long you're volunteering. The figures below reflect the current 60-day visa exemption — the Thai Cabinet approved dropping this to 30 days in May 2026 (pending official publication), which would shift these thresholds, so confirm the current rule before you rely on the exemption route:

- **Under 30 days:** Visa exemption is generally sufficient
- **30–60 days:** A tourist visa with extension typically covers this
- **60–90 days:** Tourist visa plus extension, or a Non-Immigrant O if your situation calls for it
- **90+ days:** A Non-Immigrant O is required

## Which organisations qualify?

Your sponsoring organisation must be a Thai-registered NGO, foundation, association, or state enterprise. Unregistered grassroots projects — however well-intentioned — cannot sponsor a Non-Immigrant O volunteer visa. A legitimate organisation should be able to provide registration numbers and certificates without hesitation.

## Documents required

- Passport valid for at least 6 months
- Passport-style photographs
- Proof of funds — minimum 20,000 THB
- An official sponsorship letter from your organisation, with registration certificates attached

## The 90-day stay and extension process

The Non-Immigrant O grants **90 days per entry**. Extensions require additional documentation from your organisation and a fee of **1,900 THB** per application.

## A note on orphanage volunteering

Orphanage volunteering exists in a particularly complex legal and ethical context — verifying an organisation's legitimacy matters more here than almost anywhere else in the volunteering space. We strongly recommend thorough due diligence on any organisation in this category before committing.
