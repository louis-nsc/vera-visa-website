The Destination Thailand Visa has one of the more straightforward cost structures among Thailand's long-stay visas — but there's more to budget for than just the government fee. Here's every cost involved, with a realistic first-year total.

## The government visa fee

The official DTV application fee is **10,000 THB** (approximately USD 275–300), covering the full five-year validity period. Spread across five years, that works out to roughly 2,000 THB per year — modest for what it provides.

## DTV extension fee

Each 180-day stay period can be extended a further 180 days at your local immigration office for **1,900 THB per extension**. If you stay in Thailand continuously and extend every entry, this becomes a routine, low cost compared to most alternatives.

## The big number: bank balance requirement

Applicants are generally expected to show **500,000 THB** in available funds (savings held anywhere, not necessarily in a Thai account), typically held for around three months before applying. As with the income-evidence side of the DTV, immigration doesn't publish a single fixed rule — we confirm the current expected threshold and documentation format for your specific application before you proceed.

This is evidence of financial solvency, not a deposit you hand over — the money stays yours and isn't locked into a Thai bank account the way the retirement visa's 800,000 THB requirement is.

## What's not included in the 10,000 THB fee

The government fee covers the visa itself, but not:

- **Health insurance:** roughly **25,000–60,000 THB/year** depending on age and coverage level (not legally mandatory for the DTV the way it is for the O-A, but strongly advisable)
- **Border runs:** if you choose to exit and re-enter rather than extend in-country, budget **3,000–8,000 THB per trip** for flights/transport
- **Agency fees:** if you use a visa agent for document preparation and submission, typically **3,000–7,000 THB**

## Real-world budget: first-year DTV costs

| Item | Approximate cost |
|---|---|
| Government visa fee | 10,000 THB |
| Health insurance (optional but recommended) | 25,000–60,000 THB |
| Agency support (optional) | 3,000–7,000 THB |
| **Typical first-year total** | **38,000–77,000 THB** |

That's roughly USD 1,050–2,150 for a full year of legal stay, depending on your insurance choice and whether you use an agent.

## How does the DTV compare in cost to other Thai visas?

| Visa | Approx. annual cost |
|---|---|
| DTV (extension route) | ~2,000–4,000 THB/year (fee amortised + extensions) |
| Tourist visa (repeated applications) | ~8,000 THB/year for four applications |
| Thailand Elite Card | 600,000+ THB upfront for a 5-year membership |
| Retirement visa (O-A) | ~1,900 THB/year, but requires 800,000 THB held in a Thai bank |

## DTV vs tourist visa: cost comparison over 12 months

A year of repeated 90-day tourist visas — four separate applications, plus the travel and time cost of each renewal — runs close to 8,000 THB in fees alone, before factoring in flights for border runs. The DTV's five-year validity removes that repeated-application cycle entirely.

## Is the DTV good value?

For remote workers and freelancers under 50 who don't qualify for the retirement visa, the DTV is generally the most cost-effective long-stay option available — lower ongoing cost than tourist-visa cycling, no large bank deposit requirement like the O-A, and a much lower upfront cost than the Thailand Elite Card.

## How to pay the DTV fee

The fee is paid directly as part of your application — at a Thai embassy/consulate if applying from abroad, or via the official e-visa portal if applying online. We handle fee payment as part of the application process for clients we work with.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the 500,000 THB a deposit I lose access to?** No — it's evidence of available funds, not a locked deposit. You don't hand the money over or have it held by immigration the way the retirement visa's bank deposit works.

**Do I have to buy health insurance for the DTV?** It isn't a strict legal requirement the way it is for the O-A retirement visa, but we recommend it for any extended stay in Thailand — medical costs without coverage can be significant at private hospitals.

**Is the DTV cheaper than the retirement visa?** The DTV's government fees are comparable, but it avoids the 800,000 THB Thai bank deposit the retirement visa requires — see our [DTV visa Chiang Mai guide](/dtv-visa-chiang-mai/) for the full eligibility picture.
