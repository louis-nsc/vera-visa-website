The Thai retirement visa (Non-Immigrant O-A) is one of the most accessible long-stay visas available to American retirees. It requires no Thai employer, no school enrolment, and no special skills, just proof that you can financially support yourself. This guide covers exactly what American retirees need to qualify, what income sources count, and how to apply from or through Chiang Mai.

## Who qualifies

To apply for the Thai retirement visa as a US citizen, you must:

- Be **50 years of age or older**
- Meet the financial requirements (see below)
- Have a clean criminal record (a US police background check is required)
- Have qualifying health insurance

There are no restrictions on US citizens applying for the O-A. The process is the same regardless of which state you live in.

## Financial requirements for Americans

Thai immigration accepts three methods for American applicants:

### Method 1: Bank deposit of 800,000 THB in a Thai bank

Open a Thai bank account and deposit at least 800,000 THB (approximately US$22,000–24,000 at current exchange rates). The funds must be seasoned, meaning held in the account for a set number of days before your application and kept there after.

This is the most straightforward method for Americans who want to transfer a lump sum from savings, a brokerage account, or a retirement account distribution.

### Method 2: Monthly income of 65,000 THB/month

Receive at least 65,000 THB per month (approximately US$1,800–2,000 at current rates) transferred to a Thai bank account from abroad. This must be documented with bank statements showing consistent monthly transfers.

**Does Social Security count?** Yes. Social Security income is accepted as qualifying income, provided it is transferred into your Thai bank account each month. You will need a letter from the Social Security Administration confirming your monthly benefit amount.

**Do IRA withdrawals count?** Yes, if you take regular monthly distributions. A one-time annual IRA distribution does not satisfy the monthly income method. If you take monthly distributions, you will need brokerage or custodian statements showing the amount and frequency.

**Does pension income count?** Yes. US government pensions, military pensions, and private company pensions all count, provided the income is transferred monthly to Thailand.

### Method 3: Combination

If your monthly income falls below 65,000 THB but you have savings, you can combine both. Your income plus your Thai bank balance must total 800,000 THB equivalent. For example: 40,000 THB/month income means you need at least 320,000 THB in your Thai bank to make up the difference.

## Health insurance requirements

Health insurance is mandatory for the O-A. Your policy must meet these Thai immigration minimums:

- **Outpatient:** minimum 40,000 THB coverage
- **Inpatient:** minimum 400,000 THB coverage
- Issued by a Thai-approved insurer or an overseas insurer on the approved list

Several international health insurance companies (Cigna Global, AXA, Allianz) offer policies that meet these requirements and are accepted by Thai immigration. We can refer you to options that our other US clients have used successfully.

> **Note:** US Medicare does not provide coverage in Thailand and is not accepted for visa purposes.

### Getting your FBI background check

The FBI background check is the document that takes the longest for Americans. You must submit fingerprints to the FBI Identity History Summary program. Processing takes 8–12 weeks plus mailing time. The certificate then needs to be apostilled by the US State Department.

**Start this at least 3 months before you intend to apply.** It is the most common cause of delays for our American clients.

## The application process in Chiang Mai

Most of our American clients use the in-country conversion route:

1. **Arrive in Thailand** on a tourist visa or visa exemption (Americans currently get 60 days visa-exempt; the Thai Cabinet approved dropping this to 30 days in May 2026, pending official publication, so confirm the current rule before booking)
2. **Open a Thai bank account**: Bangkok Bank and Kasikorn Bank work well for US citizens; bring your passport and a local Thai address
3. **Transfer funds** or set up recurring monthly transfers (allow deposit seasoning time)
4. **Obtain your FBI check and health insurance** before arriving, or use the time in Thailand to finalise these
5. **Apply at Chiang Mai immigration**: we attend with you and submit the full package
6. **Receive your O-A visa**: typically processed within 1–3 working days

## FATCA and US tax considerations

The US taxes its citizens on worldwide income regardless of residence. Living in Thailand on a retirement visa does not exempt you from US tax filing obligations. The US-Thailand tax treaty is limited, so consult a US expat tax specialist before making major financial moves (large transfers, retirement account withdrawals) to Thailand. ## Frequently asked questions

**Can I apply while still in the US?** Yes, at the Royal Thai Embassy in Washington DC, or at Thai consulates in New York, Chicago, Los Angeles, or Houston. You would then enter Thailand on the O-A directly.

**Can I bring my spouse?** Yes. Your spouse can apply for a dependent Non-Immigrant O based on your O-A. They must meet the same health insurance requirement and you must provide proof of marriage.

**Can I work in Thailand on the O-A?** No. The retirement visa does not permit any form of employment. You need a Non-Immigrant B visa and a work permit for that.

**What if I need to go back to the US?** Obtain a re-entry permit before you leave Thailand: single (1,000 THB) or multiple (3,800 THB). Without one, your visa is cancelled when you exit. See our [retirement visa Chiang Mai guide](/retirement-visa-chiang-mai/) for full application support.
